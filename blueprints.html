Treatment depends on the hazard. Start with the concern you have; if the hazard is unknown, use a known safe source and get local advice.

Possible fuel or toxic chemical contamination?

Do not drink or cook with this water. Boiling, settling, and improvised filters may not remove chemicals. Avoid contact and use a known safe alternative while seeking advice from local water or public-health authorities.

Recommended next steps

    Low-cost prototype concept

    A filter can improve clarity—not prove safety

    A locally built cloth or sand-and-gravel filter may reduce visible particles as a pre-treatment step. It is not a validated drinking-water treatment design and may not remove bacteria, viruses, or dissolved chemicals.

    1. Inspect and report: Note the source, location, date, and visible concern. Do not enter unsafe water or touch suspected chemicals.
    2. Separate solids only if appropriate: Allowing sediment to settle or using a clean cloth can clarify water, but does not make it safe to drink.
    3. Use a hazard-appropriate treatment: For suspected germs only, follow local public-health advice. A rolling boil can kill many disease-causing germs; it does not remove fuel or toxic chemicals.
    4. Protect treated water: Cool and store it in a clean, covered container; avoid dipping hands or cups into the stored water.
    5. Verify: Retest with an appropriate validated method or qualified laboratory. Do not label water safe based only on clarity or a single indicator.

    Close the loop: verify improvement

    After an intervention, compare before-and-after results using the same suitable test method where possible. Record who tested it, when, what was measured, and whether the result was independently verified.
