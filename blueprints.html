    Safety unknown: find a safer source
    1. Use a trusted alternative.

      Choose water from a treated supply or a source authorities have confirmed is safe. Follow any do-not-use advisory.

    2. Ask what test is appropriate.

      Tell local water or public-health authorities about the source and concern, and ask how to get it tested.

    3. Keep alternative water protected.

      Store it in a clean, tightly covered container and avoid dipping hands or used cups into it.

    Possible germs or flood contamination
    1. Check the hazard first.

      Follow local boil-water or do-not-use advisories. If fuel or chemicals may be present, do not drink or treat this water at home.

    2. For germs only, follow official treatment advice.

      If advised to boil and chemical contamination is not suspected, bring clear water to a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 feet).

    3. Cool and store it safely.

      Use a clean covered container. Keep hands, cups, and other sources of contamination out of stored water.

    4. Keep following advisories and arrange testing.

      Do not assume a source is safe based only on appearance or on one treatment step.

    Cloudy or muddy water
    1. Stop if a chemical spill is possible.

      Do not touch or use water with a fuel sheen, chemical smell, or nearby spill. Use chemical-contamination advice instead.

    2. Clarify visible particles only if appropriate.

      Let sediment settle, then carefully draw off clearer water, or strain through a clean cloth. This is only pre-treatment.

    3. Use only suitable validated treatment.

      Follow local public-health advice and product instructions for the suspected hazard. Clarifying water does not prove it is safe.

    Possible fuel or chemical contamination
    1. Stop use and avoid contact.

      Do not drink, cook, brush teeth, or wash food with suspected contaminated water. Keep children and animals away.

    2. Switch to a known safe alternative.

      Use a different source confirmed safe by the responsible authority for drinking, cooking, and personal hygiene.

    3. Report it and ask for help.

      Share the location and observation with local water or public-health authorities. Ask about testing, cleanup, and when use can resume.

    Match the action to the hazard

    No single filter solves every water problem

    The materials plan below changes with the selected concern. Low-cost cloth or settling steps are only for visible particles when chemical contamination is not suspected; they are not validated drinking-water treatment.

    1. Inspect and report: Note the source, location, date, and visible concern. Do not enter unsafe water or touch suspected chemicals.
    2. Choose a hazard-specific response: Follow local public-health advice; for a possible chemical spill, do not try a home treatment.
    3. Clarify only when appropriate: Settling or a clean cloth may reduce visible particles but does not remove all hazards or establish safety.
    4. Protect safe alternative water: Store it in a clean, covered container and avoid touching or dipping into it.
    5. Retest and seek independent review: Compare suitable results using the same method. A single indicator or a clearer appearance does not establish safety.
